As part of my Game Design degree, I have a subject called Personal Portfolio, where I can choose what I want to learn or explore based on my own interests. This gives me the opportunity to work outside the regular curriculum and develop skills that I find interesting or useful.

For my first Personal Portfolio item, I decided to learn more about sound design. During the first year of my study, I was unable to follow the Sound Design subject because I was still in the role of Engineer, which did not include this course in its curriculum. Towards the end of my first year I switched to the Designer role. Because of this change, I wanted to gain some knowledge and experience in sound design and decided to use my Personal Portfolio to follow the subject independently.

I followed the lessons and exercises from the actual Sound Design course and applied what I learned by recreating the sound of a trailer. The original trailer is approximately two minutes long, but I set myself the goal of recreating the sound for a maximum of one and a half minutes. Due to the time available for the project, I focused on completing this part of the trailer rather than the entire video.

I used Ableton to record, edit, and combine the sounds for the trailer. During the project, I learned about different aspects of sound design, including creating realistic sound effects, recording Foley, working with ambient sounds, and using audio effects in Ableton.

While working on this project, I also discovered that sound design is not something I would like to pursue further. Although I enjoyed learning the basics and gained useful knowledge that I can apply to future projects, I found the process less interesting than I initially expected. This was also a valuable result of the project, as Personal Portfolio gave me the opportunity to explore an area outside my usual interests and find out whether it was something I wanted to develop further.

Overall, I achieved what I wanted to get out of this project: I gained a basic understanding of sound design and Ableton, learned how sound can contribute to an interactive experience, and discovered that I would rather focus my future learning on other areas of game design.
